## Background and objective

Hochelaga-Maisonneuve's industrial history has left large parcels, brownfield sites, and gaps in the street network. The team examined how approximately 42 hectares of industrial land could become a mixed-use district connected to the surrounding neighbourhood and its major destinations, including the Olympic Stadium and Botanical Garden.

The proposal brings together **land-use planning, transit accessibility, and urban design**. Its central question is how a new tram connection, a finer street network, and green infrastructure could support redevelopment while addressing the site's physical constraints. It is an academic planning proposal, with future engineering and implementation requirements remaining to be resolved.

## Site analysis

The site analysis combined **GIS mapping, land-use observation, historical research, and ecological context**. Historic development along Notre-Dame Street, former agricultural divisions, industrial buildings, and rail infrastructure help explain the present parcel pattern. The loss of historic waterways and extensive hard surfaces informed the report's attention to stormwater and green infrastructure.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/uploads/revitalizing-hochelaga-maisonneuve/final-perm.jpg" alt="Historical and ecological analysis map of the study area." caption="Historical and ecological features used to interpret site conditions." %}

Large industrial plots and limited vegetation create both redevelopment opportunities and constraints. The report identifies manufacturing, storage, and distribution uses associated with the area's industrial and port functions. Reuse would need to address these existing activities alongside environmental remediation and connections to adjacent residential areas.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/uploads/revitalizing-hochelaga-maisonneuve/Copy%20of%20URBS%20433%20FINAL%20REPORT%20MAY%201ST-3.jpg" alt="Satellite-based analysis of industrial parcels and brownfield land." caption="Industrial parcels and brownfield context within the study area." %}

## Redevelopment vision

The vision is a district where housing, employment, public space, and everyday services reinforce one another. The proposal extends the surrounding street fabric into large industrial parcels and uses transit access to support a denser mix of uses.

- **Transit-oriented development:** connect the site to a proposed tram line and the broader transit network.
- **Urban integration:** replace isolated large parcels with smaller blocks and connected walking routes.
- **Public realm and green infrastructure:** combine gathering spaces, vegetation, and stormwater considerations.
- **Everyday activity:** pair housing and employment with commercial, cultural, and recreational destinations that can support use beyond major events.

## Slope feasibility

**Slope profiling** was used to compare L'Assomption Boulevard and Viau Street. The study's charts use a 6% reference threshold and show sections of L'Assomption approaching 14%. These profiles informed the comparison of surface routes and grade-separated options; they do not establish final engineering feasibility.

The team proposed a tunnel segment on Viau to manage steep sections and limit disruption at the surface. This connects terrain analysis to a planning decision: where the alignment should run, and where surface infrastructure may be difficult to accommodate.

## Transit coverage

The team used **500-metre buffers** to compare the proposed tram stops with existing métro coverage. The report identifies areas that could gain access around Ontario Street, Pierre-de-Coubertin, and the southern portion of the study area. Buffer coverage provides a spatial screening measure; it should not be read as a measured journey time or a detailed pedestrian network model.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/uploads/revitalizing-hochelaga-maisonneuve/stops.jpg" alt="Proposed tram stops shown alongside the existing transit network." caption="Proposed stop locations and their relationship to existing transit." %}
{% include figure.html src="/assets/uploads/revitalizing-hochelaga-maisonneuve/Coverage.jpg" alt="Coverage map showing 500-metre zones around proposed tram and existing metro stops." caption="The report's 500-metre transit coverage analysis." %}

The report also relates the alignment to Montréal's 2022 climate sensitivity mapping. Green tracks and planted corridors are proposed as part of a mobility strategy that considers heat and stormwater alongside access to schools, parks, and commercial areas.

## Land use

The land-use plan combines a predominantly residential district with mixed-use, institutional, industrial, and commercial functions. A proposed commercial corridor along Pierre-de-Coubertin connects to the tram and shared street. Industrial and institutional uses are concentrated toward the north to manage their relationship with housing.

## Building heights

The proposed height strategy ranges from **3–4 storeys for residential buildings** to **5–6 storeys for mixed-use buildings**, with **11–12-storey buildings** at selected edges near green spaces. The report describes a land-use mix of 64% residential, 21% mixed-use, 7% institutional, 5% industrial, and 3% commercial.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/uploads/revitalizing-hochelaga-maisonneuve/Building_h.jpg" alt="Map of proposed building heights across the redevelopment site." caption="Proposed building heights and their spatial distribution." %}

The report's narrative projects **12,574 new dwellings** and a **population increase of 31,220**. Its land-use table separately lists **13,574 total dwellings**. These source labels are preserved here; the report does not reconcile the difference between the two dwelling figures. All figures describe the proposed scenario, rather than completed development.
