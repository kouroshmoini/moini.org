## Research focus

This paper examines the Muskrat Falls hydroelectric project through **infrastructure financing, public risk exposure, and megaproject governance**. It considers how early cost estimates, oversight arrangements, delivery structures, and political decision-making contributed to cost overruns and long-term financial burdens.

The case study connects project finance to broader planning questions: how risk is allocated, how decisions are scrutinized, and how public institutions remain accountable over the life of a major infrastructure project.

## Analytical themes

- **Cost estimation:** the relationship between early project estimates and subsequent cost overruns.
- **Financial risk:** public ownership, federal loan guarantees, and the allocation of financial exposure.
- **Governance and accountability:** oversight, fragmented delivery structures, and political decision-making.
- **Environmental and community considerations:** environmental concerns associated with hydroelectric development and Indigenous consultation.

## Planning implications

The paper brings financial and governance questions into the same discussion as environmental concerns and consultation. These themes demonstrate an approach to infrastructure research that considers both project delivery and its wider public consequences.
